## IDENTIFICATION OF ISSUES

**I.** Whether there are substantial grounds to believe that General Roger Thorn is individually criminally responsible under Article 25(3)(c) of the Rome Statute for aiding, abetting or otherwise assisting in the commission, or attempted commission, of the war crime of torture or inhuman treatment, including biological experiments, under Article 8(2)(a)(ii), in relation to the injections of Jaku on the Titan soldiers between 2 and 10 April 2025 (Count 1).

## STATEMENT OF FACTS

**Background.** The Republic of Pallavon is a small landlocked State created by the 1905 Declaration of Peace between Damaroi and Bhumara. It maintains purely defensive capabilities, including the Sky-2-Rock ["S2R"] close-in missile defence system. [SAF 2–5, 30] In October 2024, the National Reclamation Party won Damaroi's elections, and its leader, Aran Veyar, became President after pledging to reintegrate Pallavon by "any means necessary". [SAF 8–10]

**Operation Titan.** On 3 February 2025, President Veyar tasked General Roger Thorn, Chief of the Damaran Defence Force, with dismantling Pallavon's aerial defence systems "with the objective to leave Pallavon vulnerable for Damaroi to take control of the country". Thorn hand-picked a platoon of 50 elite soldiers, the "Titans". [SAF 11] On 10 March 2025, Thorn led them into the Damaran Gap; after the fifth day, they crossed the River Targa into Pallavon territory. [SAF 12] Disease stalled the operation and five soldiers died. On 19 March, Thorn, himself ill, ordered the five healthiest soldiers to continue through the Gap and retreated with the rest to Damaroi, setting fire to the Pallavon bank of the River Targa "for 'easier access next time'". [SAF 13]

**The Jaku injections (Count 1).** All 40 returning platoon members contracted an unknown disease with severe symptoms, and four were placed in induced comas. [SAF 15] At the hospital, Thorn told the President: "When we go back in, we need to be stronger." Veyar offered a "boost" to make them "even mightier than last time". [SAF 16] Veyar then approached Dr Nora Loff of the Starr Institute about a "top-secret matter of national security". Dr Loff stated that the experimental drug Jaku could restore the soldiers' health, enhance their immunity, and increase their muscle strength and endurance. Although it was in the "early stages of development", she assured Veyar that it was "ready for human trials" and, if it worked, "would put Damaroi back on the map as a leader in biomedical sciences". Veyar passed on all of this information to Thorn, who agreed "without hesitation" and proposed that it be given to "the whole group". [SAF 17] From 2 April, Dr Loff administered half doses of Jaku to all platoon members, including Thorn. The four comatose soldiers were treated without their consent; the conscious soldiers verbally agreed. Many soldiers then reported severe headaches, disorientation and episodes of paranoia. [SAF 18] Dr Loff assured Thorn that the symptoms were temporary and suggested increasing the dosage "for optimal results". Thorn, "having experienced newfound strength himself", gave his approval "on behalf of the Titans", and Dr Loff moved to full doses without notifying or obtaining further consent from any of the soldiers. [SAF 19] All 40 soldiers regained full health, with performance "that exceeded expectations", but they continued to experience episodes of disorientation and paranoia. On 10 April, despite these side-effects, the group was deemed fit and the injections stopped. [SAF 20] After a leak, Veyar publicly claimed that the treatment was "for rehabilitation only". [SAF 22]

**Operation Titan 2.0 and the hostilities.** On 1 June 2025, Thorn led Operation Titan 2.0 back into Pallavon. [SAF 24] The Titans took control of the town of Santo, [SAF 25–27] and Pallavon ordered mass conscription, with Bhumara supplying resources. [SAF 28–29] On 1 July, the Titans destroyed a Pallavon radar installation, and Damaroi then launched missiles from its own territory against Pallavon's training facilities. [SAF 31–34] On 4 July, Veyar told Thorn to be Damaroi's "eyes and ears" and to get him "sound intel and a plan". [SAF 35]

**The schools (Count 2).** On 5 July, Pallavon's Cabinet decided to repurpose schools of an area greater than 2 hectares and within 1 kilometre of the train network as new sites to train recruits and prepare operations. Of the 20 schools fitting that description, 12 were suspended and converted into military facilities. [SAF 37] The Titans gathered detailed intelligence on the converted schools, [SAF 38–39] and on Thorn's recommendation they were struck on 13–14 July. [SAF 40] A pattern followed: each time a facility was targeted, another school of the same size and location was evacuated and converted. Five further schools were converted, monitored by the Titans, and struck after their conversion was confirmed. [SAF 41] On 20 July, the remaining three schools were dismissed from teaching, but "no positive action had yet been taken to use the premises for military purposes". Thorn nevertheless "gave the order to target all remaining schools fitting the criteria, and provided strike coordinates". When Veyar observed, "We don't have any confirmation the schools are actually being used as facilities", Thorn replied: "The pattern is obvious … Even if they are not being used now, their purpose is clear. Plus, the grounds have already been evacuated. Our best course of action would be to destroy them before Pallavon has the chance [to] use them against us." He proposed that the operation be carried out at night. [SAF 42] At 1:10 am on 22 July, a barrage of missiles was launched from Como City "to coordinates provided by Thorn". Most were intercepted. Two schools were hit once and partially damaged, and the third was obliterated, killing cleaning staff and severely damaging surrounding workplaces and roads. [SAF 43]

**Proceedings.** Thorn was captured on 31 July 2025. [SAF 52] On 20 May 2026, Pre-Trial Chamber I issued a summons to appear, and Thorn made his initial appearance on 15 June 2026, maintaining his innocence. [SAF 55–56]

## SUMMARY OF PLEADINGS

**Count 1.** There are substantial grounds to believe that Thorn aided, abetted or otherwise assisted the war crime of inhuman treatment, including biological experiments. Jaku was in the "early stages of development" and was administered to test its unknown effects, including enhancement of strength and endurance, and its results were measured against expectations. The experiment seriously endangered the Titans' health: side-effects of headaches, disorientation and paranoia were known before the dose was raised, and they persisted. Its intent was non-therapeutic. The dose was raised "for optimal results" after Thorn felt "newfound strength", and the injections stopped at combat fitness, not at recovery. The comatose soldiers could not consent, no one consented to the higher dose, and Thorn's approval "on behalf of the Titans" could not substitute for their consent. The Titans, as wounded and sick members of the armed forces, were protected under GC I, and Thorn knew the facts giving them that status. The injections prepared the Titans for Operation Titan 2.0 in an international armed conflict that began when Damaran forces entered Pallavon on 10 March 2025. By approving the treatment for the whole group and then approving the increased dosage, Thorn assisted Dr Loff with knowledge of the drug's experimental nature, its side-effects and the absence of consent.

**Count 2.** There are substantial grounds to believe that Thorn ordered, or alternatively solicited or induced, the war crime of intentionally directing attacks against civilian objects. The three schools were civilian objects. Article 52(3) of AP I presumes that schools are not used for military purposes in case of doubt, and that doubt was acknowledged by the President himself. No positive action had been taken to use the schools militarily, their size and location showed only suitability, and any future use depended on contingencies, so they made no effective contribution to military action and their destruction offered no definite military advantage. Military necessity cannot justify attacking civilian objects. Thorn selected the schools, provided the coordinates and urged their destruction while admitting they were "not being used now". He therefore intended them to be the object of the attack. He "gave the order", and the missiles were fired at his coordinates; alternatively, he induced the attack by overcoming the President's hesitation. He made no mistake of fact, any mistake of law does not negate intent, and the record does not show that Jaku destroyed his capacity.

### A. GENERAL THORN IS INDIVIDUALLY CRIMINALLY RESPONSIBLE UNDER ARTICLE 25(3)(c) FOR AIDING, ABETTING OR OTHERWISE ASSISTING IN THE COMMISSION OF THE WAR CRIME OF TORTURE OR INHUMAN TREATMENT, INCLUDING BIOLOGICAL EXPERIMENTS, UNDER ARTICLE 8(2)(a)(ii) (COUNT 1)

#### 1. The Titans were subjected to a particular biological experiment

The first element is satisfied because [a] Jaku's administration falls within the meaning of a "biological experiment", and [b] the Titans were subjected to it.⁹


⁹ ICC, Elements of Crimes ["EoC"] art 8(2)(a)(ii)-3, Element 1.  


**a. The meaning of "biological experiment"**

In its ordinary meaning, a biological experiment is conduct whose primary purpose is to study the effects, at that time unknown, of a product or situation on the human body.¹⁰ Articles 12 of the First and Second Geneva Conventions prohibit "biological experiments", while Article 13 of the Third and Article 32 of the Fourth Geneva Conventions prohibit "medical or scientific experiments".¹¹ The grave breach provisions of all four Conventions use "biological experiments", which shows that the two concepts overlap considerably.¹² The corresponding customary rule is Rule 92 of the ICRC Customary IHL Study.¹³

The prohibition is rooted in post-Second World War practice. The Medical Trial, *Hoess* and *Milch* addressed experiments on human beings,¹⁴ and the programme of the Imperial Japanese Army is the paradigm historical case.¹⁵ Scholarship defines experimentation as systematic intervention or observation that seeks generalisable information from or about a human being, and finds no meaningful distinction between "biological" and "medical" research.¹⁶ Whichever term is used, the core of the definition remains the same: the conduct tests effects that are "at that time unknown".

Whether conduct is genuinely directed at a stated objective is assessed objectively. In *Whaling in the Antarctic*, the International Court of Justice asked whether a programme's "design and implementation are reasonable in relation to achieving its stated objectives".¹⁷ Applied by analogy, the restoration of health alone cannot justify raising the dose "for optimal results".¹⁸ Further, the sick cannot validly consent to a biological experiment.¹⁹

#### 3. The intent of the experiment was non-therapeutic, it was not medically justified, and it was not carried out in the Titans' interest

The third element is satisfied because [a] the legal framework permits only experiments justified by the person's medical treatment and carried out in his interest; [b] the purpose of Jaku was enhancement, not cure; [c] there was no medical justification and the experiment was not in the soldiers' interest; [d] Thorn need not have made the value judgement himself; and [e] consent does not save the experiment.


**a. The legal framework**

Only experiments justified by the person's medical treatment and carried out in his interest are permitted. **c. There was no medical justification, and the experiment was not in the Titans' interest**

#### 5. The conduct took place in the context of and was associated with an international armed conflict, of which Thorn was aware

Elements 6 and 7 are satisfied because [a] an international armed conflict ["IAC"] existed between Damaroi and Pallavon; [b] Thorn's conduct was associated with it; and [c] he was aware of the factual circumstances establishing it.


**a. An international armed conflict existed between Damaroi and Pallavon from 10 March 2025**

Common Article 2 applies to any armed conflict between two or more High Contracting Parties, even if one of them does not recognise a state of war, and no particular threshold of violence is required.⁹⁰ An IAC exists whenever there is a "resort to armed force between States".⁹¹ It arises when one State has recourse to armed force against another, regardless of the reasons for or the intensity of the confrontation; no declaration or recognition is required, and its existence depends on the facts on the ground.⁹² An IAC also exists in the case of "armed hostilities between States through their respective armed forces or other actors acting on behalf of the State".⁹³ Any difference between two States leading to the intervention of armed forces is an armed conflict, whatever its duration or the number of victims,⁹⁴ and it is irrelevant whether the attacked State resists.⁹⁵ IHL applies throughout the territory of the parties, not only in the vicinity of actual hostilities,⁹⁶ so the injections administered in Damaroi fall within the IAC.

On 3 February 2025, the President tasked Thorn, Chief of the Damaran Defence Force, with dismantling Pallavon's aerial defence systems in order to leave Pallavon vulnerable "for Damaroi to take control of the country".⁹⁷ On 10 March, Thorn led armed Damaran soldiers into the Damaran Gap, and they crossed the River Targa into Pallavon territory.⁹⁸ Thorn ordered five soldiers to continue the mission inside Pallavon and, as he retreated, set fire to the Pallavon bank of the River Targa "for 'easier access next time'".⁹⁹ This was a resort to armed force by Damaroi against Pallavon's territory. The conflict then intensified: the Titans seized Santo, destroyed a Pallavon radar installation, and Damaroi launched missile strikes from its own territory.¹⁰⁰ As such, an IAC existed from 10 March 2025 and continued through July 2025.

Civilian objects are all objects which are not military objectives.¹³¹ An object is a military objective only if, cumulatively, [i] by its nature, location, purpose or use it makes an effective contribution to military action, and [ii] its total or partial destruction, capture or neutralisation, in the circumstances ruling at the time, offers a definite military advantage.¹³² This definition reflects customary international law.¹³³ The schools remained civilian objects because [a] the presumption in Article 52(3) protected them; [b] they made no effective contribution to military action; [c] their destruction offered no definite military advantage; and [d] military necessity cannot fill the gap.


¹³¹ AP I (n 70) art 52(1); EoC (n 9) art 8(2)(b)(ii), Element 2.  
¹³² AP I (n 70) art 52(2); Dörmann in Triffterer (n 10) mn 210–211.  
¹³³ CIHL (n 13) Rules 8–10; Eritrea-Ethiopia Claims Commission, *Partial Award: Western Front, Aerial Bombardment and Related Claims – Eritrea's Claims 1, 3, 5, 9–13, 14, 21, 25 & 26* (19 December 2005) XXVI RIAA 291 ["EECC Partial Award"] [113]; ICTY, *Final Report to the Prosecutor by the Committee Established to Review the NATO Bombing Campaign Against the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia* (13 June 2000) 39 ILM 1257 [42].  


**a. The schools were protected by the presumption in Article 52(3)**

In case of doubt whether an object normally dedicated to civilian purposes, "such as a place of worship, a house or other dwelling or a school", is being used to make an effective contribution to military action, it shall be presumed not to be so used.¹³⁴ The drafters named schools expressly.¹³⁵ The presumption must be given effect: *ut res magis valeat quam pereat*. It would lose all meaning if an attacker could displace it simply by pointing to a school's size or its suitability for conversion. The burden of justifying an attack lies on the party that launches it.¹³⁶ The doubt here was acknowledged at the highest level: President Veyar said, "We don't have any confirmation the schools are actually being used as facilities."¹³⁷

Schools receive special protection in international law. The Rome Statute separately criminalises attacks on buildings dedicated to education,¹³⁸ the Security Council has repeatedly condemned attacks on schools,¹³⁹ and over 100 States have endorsed the Safe Schools Declaration.¹⁴⁰ Dismissing the pupils on 20 July cannot rebut the presumption.¹⁴¹ Pallavon was obliged to remove civilians from the vicinity of likely targets.¹⁴² Removing pupils from schools of the very profile Damaroi had repeatedly struck was exactly such a precaution. A defending State's compliance with international humanitarian law cannot be turned into evidence of military use.

**b. The schools made no effective contribution to military action**

An object's contribution must be effective "in the actual situation, not in abstracto"; otherwise, every object potentially of use to enemy troops would become a military objective.¹⁴³ The three schools contributed nothing by [i] their nature or use, [ii] their location, or [iii] their purpose.


¹⁴³ van Houtte Opinion (n 136) [3].  


***i. Nature or use***

Schools are civilian by nature, and "use" concerns an object's present function.¹⁴⁴ On 20 July, "no positive action had yet been taken to use the premises for military purposes".¹⁴⁵ The contrast with the earlier seventeen schools is stark. Those sites showed recruits, combat drills, deliveries of rifles and ammunition, and mapped barracks,¹⁴⁶ and they were struck only "following the confirmation of their conversion".¹⁴⁷ No comparable indicator existed for the final three schools. Thorn himself conceded: "Even if they are not being used now".¹⁴⁸ *Habemus optimum testem, confitentem reum*: the best witness is an accused who admits the fact.

***ii. Location***

Contribution by location concerns objects that have no military function but whose position itself has military value, such as a bridge.¹⁴⁹ The Cabinet's criteria of "an area greater than 2 hectares and within 1 kilometre from the train network"¹⁵⁰ describe a school's suitability for conversion, not a present military function of the site. If suitability were enough, every large hospital, stadium or university near a railway line would be a lawful target. The law rejects that result.¹⁵¹

***iii. Purpose***

Purpose concerns the intended future use of an object.¹⁵² There must be concrete indicators of future military use; mere suspicion is not sufficient.¹⁵³ The facts disclose no such indicators. First, the Cabinet criteria did not allocate every qualifying school to military use: of the 20 schools meeting the criteria, only 12 were initially converted.¹⁵⁴ Secondly, later conversions were reactive: "each time a facility was targeted, another school of the same size and location was evacuated and converted".¹⁵⁵ Any future use of the final three schools therefore depended on future Damaran strikes and future decisions by Pallavon. Thirdly, Thorn relied on a "pattern", not on intelligence about these three sites.¹⁵⁶ His own words, that the schools should be destroyed "before Pallavon has the chance [to] use them against us", admit that no such use had yet occurred.¹⁵⁷ Where the facts support both a protective inference and a military one, Article 52(3) requires the protective one.¹⁵⁸

**c. The destruction of the schools offered no definite military advantage**

A "definite" military advantage must be concrete and perceptible, not hypothetical and speculative.¹⁵⁹ An object may not be attacked where it offers only potential or indeterminate advantages.¹⁶⁰ The words "in the circumstances ruling at the time" confirm that an object's status is judged at the moment of attack, not by what might happen later.¹⁶¹ Here, the claimed advantage depended on a chain of contingencies: Pallavon would first have to decide to convert the schools, then take steps to repurpose them, and then use them in support of military operations. Such an advantage is speculative by definition.¹⁶²

The Defence may rely on the Eritrea-Ethiopia Claims Commission's statement that military advantage is assessed in the context of the military operations "taken as a whole".¹⁶³ That reliance is misplaced. The Commission was dealing with a power station, a type of object "generally recognized" as a military objective, and it still treated effective contribution as a separate "first step".¹⁶⁴ Schools are the opposite: they are presumptively civilian. Further, the "attack as a whole" concept refers to a finite military operation and must not be confused with winning the entire war.¹⁶⁵ As such, the second limb of Article 52(2) is not satisfied.

**d. Military necessity cannot justify an attack on civilian objects**

The prohibition on attacking civilian objects is absolute, and military necessity cannot be invoked to justify breaching it.¹⁶⁶ It "may not be derogated from because of military necessity".¹⁶⁷ The Nuremberg tribunals rejected the doctrine of *Kriegsraison geht vor Kriegsmanier* ("necessity in war overrides the rules of war").¹⁶⁸ Military necessity was already taken into account when the targeting rules were drafted, so it cannot be invoked a second time to override them.¹⁶⁹ Since 1868, the only legitimate object of war has been to weaken the military forces of the enemy.¹⁷⁰ Thorn's logic was to destroy schools before they could be used. Applied generally, it would make every civilian object with any conceivable military utility a target, and it would erase the principle of distinction, which the International Court of Justice has described as "cardinal" and "intransgressible".¹⁷¹

Even under the *Rendulic* standard, a commander's conduct is judged on the information available to him at the time.¹⁷² The information before Thorn was that there was no confirmation of military use.¹⁷³ His proposal of a night strike did not cure this defect. A night-time operation may reduce the expected presence of civilians, but it cannot transform a civilian object into a military objective. The resulting deaths of cleaning staff and the damage to surrounding workplaces and roads are not elements of Count 2, but they show the consequences of the operation.¹⁷⁴ As such, the three schools were civilian objects.

#### 3. Thorn directed an attack against the schools and intended them to be its object

Elements 1 and 3 are satisfied because [a] an attack was directed against the schools, and [b] Thorn intended the schools to be its object, [c] an inference reinforced by his departure from feasible verification.


**a. An attack was directed against the schools**

An "attack" means acts of violence against the adversary, whether in offence or in defence.¹⁷⁵ The barrage of missiles launched from Como City on 22 July was an attack.¹⁷⁶ The crime is a conduct crime: no particular result is required, and the attack is complete even if the weapon fails to hit its target.¹⁷⁷ The interception of most missiles is therefore irrelevant; in any event, all three schools were hit.¹⁷⁸ The schools were not damaged incidentally during an attack on another objective. They were the intended objects of the operation: Thorn ordered that "all remaining schools fitting the criteria" be targeted and provided the strike coordinates.¹⁷⁹ This distinguishes Count 2 from Article 8(2)(b)(iv), which concerns excessive incidental harm caused by attacks on lawful military objectives.¹⁸⁰

**b. Thorn intended the schools to be the object of the attack**

The perpetrator must have intended the civilian objects to be the object of the attack.¹⁸¹ Under Article 30, he must have meant to engage in the conduct and, in relation to a consequence, meant to cause it or been aware that it would occur in the ordinary course of events.¹⁸² The intent must relate to the object of the attack, not merely to the act of launching it.¹⁸³ Thorn "gave the order to target all remaining schools fitting the criteria", provided the strike coordinates, and said that the best course of action would be "to destroy them".¹⁸⁴ At the very least, he was aware that their destruction would occur in the ordinary course of events.¹⁸⁵

Thorn also knew the facts relevant to the schools' civilian status. An attack on civilian property is criminal where it is launched with knowledge, or where it was impossible not to know, that civilian property was being targeted.¹⁸⁶ Thorn was told that there was no confirmation of military use; he responded, "Even if they are not being used now, their purpose is clear"; and he noted that "the grounds have already been evacuated".¹⁸⁷ These statements show that he knew present military use was unconfirmed and nevertheless sought the schools' destruction on the basis of anticipated future use. *Res ipsa loquitur*. His proposal of a night-time operation does not negate intent; it confirms that the school premises themselves were deliberately selected as the targets.¹⁸⁸

**c. Thorn's departure from feasible verification reinforces the inference of intent**

Those who plan or decide upon an attack must do everything feasible to verify that the objectives to be attacked are neither civilian objects nor subject to special protection,¹⁸⁹ and must cancel or suspend an attack if it becomes apparent that the objective is not a military one.¹⁹⁰ An object may not be attacked where it is not reasonable to believe, on the information available, that it is being used to make an effective contribution to military action.¹⁹¹ Thorn fell within this category, because he selected the schools, urged their destruction and supplied the coordinates.¹⁹² The Titans' earlier practice shows that verification was feasible: after the strikes of 13–14 July, they observed and monitored five further schools, and the attacks followed "the confirmation of their conversion".¹⁹³ For the final three schools, Thorn departed from that practice and urged destruction despite the President's express observation that there was no confirmation.¹⁹⁴ This departure supports the inference that he deliberately selected the schools for preventive destruction.

#### 4. Thorn is individually criminally responsible under Article 25(3)(b)

Thorn is responsible because he [a] ordered the attack or, alternatively, [b] solicited or induced it.


**a. Primary mode: Thorn ordered the attack**

Ordering involves a person in a position of authority who instructs another to commit a crime, where the order has a direct effect on the commission of the crime and the person is at least aware that the crime will be committed in the ordinary course of events.¹⁹⁵ A formal superior–subordinate relationship is not indispensable where the accused has sufficient authority to direct the commission of the crime.¹⁹⁶ An order may be proved by circumstantial evidence.¹⁹⁷ A person who orders a crime commits it through another and must share the required intent: *qui facit per alium facit per se*.¹⁹⁸

Thorn was Chief of the Damaran Defence Force.¹⁹⁹ He led the Titan operation in Pallavon and was tasked by the President to act as Damaroi's "eyes and ears", to gather "sound intel and a plan", and to "figure out what that looks like".²⁰⁰ On 20 July, he "gave the order to target all remaining schools fitting the criteria, and provided strike coordinates".²⁰¹ His instruction was not abstract advice: it identified the targets and supplied the coordinates the missile operation required. Two days later, a barrage of missiles was launched from Como City "to coordinates provided by Thorn".²⁰² This sequence shows that his order had a direct effect on the attack, and that he was at least aware the attack would occur in the ordinary course of events. The crime "in fact occur[red]", and, in respect of the intercepted missiles, was at least "attempted".²⁰³

**b. Alternative mode: Thorn solicited or induced the attack**

Soliciting means urging, advising, commanding or otherwise inciting another to commit a crime; inducing means enticing or urging another to do so.²⁰⁴ Both concern conduct by which a person influences another to commit a crime, and neither requires a superior–subordinate relationship.²⁰⁵ President Veyar questioned the strike because there was no confirmation that the schools were being used as military facilities.²⁰⁶ Thorn responded that "[t]he pattern is obvious", urged that the schools be destroyed "before Pallavon has the chance [to] use them against us", and provided the strike coordinates.²⁰⁷ His conduct was directed at securing the strike despite the acknowledged absence of confirmation. #### 5. No ground excludes Thorn's criminal responsibility

No ground excludes Thorn's responsibility because [a] he made no mistake of fact; [b] any mistake of law does not negate his intent; and [c] the record does not establish that Jaku destroyed his capacity.

A mistake of fact excludes responsibility only if it negates the mental element required by the crime.²⁰⁹ The recognised example is a commander who acts in good faith on erroneous information and attacks an object he believes to be a military objective.²¹⁰ Thorn is the opposite case. He was not misinformed: he knew that there was no confirmation, acknowledged that the schools were "not being used now", and noted that the grounds had been evacuated.²¹¹

**c. The record does not establish that Jaku destroyed Thorn's capacity**

Article 31(1)(a) and (b) require that a mental disease, defect or intoxication destroy the person's capacity to appreciate the unlawfulness or nature of his conduct, or his capacity to control it.²¹⁴ This is consistent with Count 1: that Jaku seriously endangered the Titans' mental health does not mean that it destroyed Thorn's capacity. SAF [39](g) reports that "[a]ll Titans reported varied degrees of paranoia"; it does not identify the extent to which any such symptoms affected Thorn at the time of the targeting decision.²¹⁵ By early July, Thorn told the President that he could feel the benefits of Jaku "wearing off".²¹⁶ His conduct on 20 July was coherent and goal-directed: he identified a pattern, answered the President's concern, proposed a night operation "to minimise risk of harming civilians", and provided strike coordinates.²¹⁷ A person who weighs the risk to civilians plainly appreciates the nature of his conduct. The President's later remark that Thorn's proposed attack on Barna Depot "doesn't sound like you" came after the school strike and concerned a separate operation.²¹⁸ As such, no ground excludes Thorn's criminal responsibility.

Thus, there are substantial grounds to believe that Thorn is individually criminally responsible under Article 25(3)(b) for the war crime under Article 8(2)(b)(ii).
