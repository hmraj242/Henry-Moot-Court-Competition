# **STATEMENT OF JURISDICTION** {#statement-of-jurisdiction .unnumbered}

The Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court \["the Prosecution"\]
respectfully appears before Pre-Trial Chamber I of the International
Criminal Court \["the Court"\] in the Situation in the Republic of
Pallavon. The Republic of Pallavon and the Republic of Damaroi are both
States Parties to the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court
\["the Rome Statute"\], and the conduct charged occurred on their
territories. The Court therefore has jurisdiction under Articles 5, 8,
12(2)(a) and 13(c) of the Rome Statute. On 28 April 2026, Pre-Trial
Chamber I authorized the Prosecutor's *proprio motu* investigation
pursuant to Article 15(4) of the Rome Statute and Rule 50(5) of the
Rules of Procedure and Evidence. On 20 May 2026, the Chamber issued a
summons to appear for General Roger Thorn pursuant to Article 58(7) of
the Rome Statute, and he made his initial appearance on 15 June 2026
pursuant to Article 60(1) of the Rome Statute and Rule 121(1) of the
Rules of Procedure and Evidence. The present hearing is held pursuant to
Article 61 of the Rome Statute to confirm the charges set out in the
Document Containing the Charges.

# **IDENTIFICATION OF ISSUES** {#identification-of-issues .unnumbered}

**I.** Whether there are substantial grounds to believe that the accused
is individually criminally responsible under Article 25(3)(c) of the
Rome Statute for aiding, abetting or otherwise assisting in the
commission or attempted commission of the war crime of torture or
inhuman treatment, including biological experiments, under Article
8(2)(a)(ii), in respect of the injections of Jaku on Titan soldiers
between 2 and 10 April 2025.

**II.** Whether there are substantial grounds to believe that General
Roger Thorn is individually criminally responsible under Article
25(3)(b) of the Rome Statute for ordering, soliciting or inducing the
war crime of intentionally directing attacks against civilian objects
under Article 8(2)(b)(ii), in respect of the missile strike on three
schools on 22 July 2025.

# **STATEMENT OF FACTS** {#statement-of-facts .unnumbered}

In October 2024, Aran Veyar became President of Damaroi, pledging to
reintegrate Pallavon by "any means necessary". On 3 February 2025, he
tasked General Roger Thorn, Chief of the Damaran Defence Force, with
dismantling Pallavon's aerial defences so that Damaroi could take
control of it. On 15 March, Thorn led 50 hand-picked soldiers, the
"Titans", into Pallavon. After disease killed five, he withdrew with 40,
all severely ill; four were placed in induced comas. In hospital, Thorn
told Veyar, "When we go back in, we need to be stronger." Dr Nora Loff
of the Starr Institute proposed Jaku, an experimental drug in the "early
stages of development" said to restore health and increase strength.
Thorn agreed "without hesitation" for "the whole group". From 2 April,
Dr Loff injected half doses into all 40: the comatose four without
consent, the others on verbal agreement. After many suffered headaches,
disorientation and paranoia, Thorn, feeling "newfound strength",
approved a higher dose "on behalf of the Titans", and Dr Loff moved to
full doses without telling any soldier. Injections stopped on 10 April.
All 40 recovered, but disorientation and paranoia persisted. On 1 June,
the Titans re-entered Pallavon, seized Santo and destroyed a radar
installation, while Damaran missiles targeted Pallavon's training
facilities. On 5 July, Pallavon's Cabinet decided to convert large
schools near the railway into military training sites. Twelve of the 20
qualifying schools were converted and, on Thorn's recommendation, struck
on 13--14 July; five more were struck only after the Titans confirmed
their conversion. On 20 July, the last three stopped teaching, but "no
positive action had yet been taken to use the premises for military
purposes". Thorn nevertheless ordered them targeted and provided strike
coordinates. When Veyar warned that military use was unconfirmed, Thorn
replied that "\[e\]ven if they are not being used now, their purpose is
clear", and proposed a night strike. At 1:10 am on 22 July, missiles
launched from Como City, Damaroi's capital, struck Thorn's coordinates,
partially damaging two schools and destroying the third, killing
cleaning staff.

# **SUMMARY OF PLEADINGS** {#summary-of-pleadings .unnumbered}

## **Count 1: Thorn aided and abetted the Jaku biological experiments on the Titans** {#count-1-thorn-aided-and-abetted-the-jaku-biological-experiments-on-the-titans .unnumbered}

There are substantial grounds to believe that Thorn aided, abetted or
otherwise assisted the war crime of inhuman treatment, including
biological experiments. Dr Loff subjected the Titans to Jaku, an
experimental drug in the "early stages of development" whose unknown
effects were tested on all 40 soldiers. The experiment seriously
endangered their health because severe side effects were known before
the dose was increased and persisted afterwards. Its purpose was
enhancement rather than cure, since the dose was raised "for optimal
results" after some soldiers had already recovered, and no soldier gave
valid consent to it. The Titans were sick members of the armed forces
protected under GC I, and Thorn knew the facts establishing that status.
The injections were associated with an international armed conflict that
began when Damaran forces crossed into Pallavon in March 2025. By
approving Jaku for "the whole group" and then the increased dose "on
behalf of the Titans", Thorn assisted Dr Loff knowing of the drug's
experimental nature, its side effects and the absence of consent.

## **Count 2: Thorn ordered the missile attack on three civilian schools** {#count-2-thorn-ordered-the-missile-attack-on-three-civilian-schools .unnumbered}

There are substantial grounds to believe that Thorn ordered or,
alternatively, induced the war crime of intentionally directing attacks
against civilian objects. The three schools were civilian objects. No
positive action had been taken to use them militarily, and the
presumption in Article 52(3) of AP I applies because the President
himself acknowledged that their use was unconfirmed. Their size and
location showed only suitability for future use, so they made no
effective contribution to military action and their destruction offered
no definite military advantage. Damaran forces directed the attack
against the schools, deliberately targeting them at coordinates selected
by Thorn. Thorn ordered the strike knowing that military use was
unconfirmed, and his departure from the Titans' practice of striking
only after confirmation shows that he knew civilian objects would be
attacked. Alternatively, he induced the attack by overcoming the
President's hesitation. No mistake of fact or law negates his mental
element, and nothing shows that Jaku destroyed his capacity.

# **PLEADINGS** {#pleadings .unnumbered}

## **GENERAL THORN IS INDIVIDUALLY CRIMINALLY RESPONSIBLE UNDER ARTICLE 25(3)(c) FOR AIDING, ABETTING OR OTHERWISE ASSISTING IN THE COMMISSION OF THE WAR CRIME OF TORTURE OR INHUMAN TREATMENT, INCLUDING BIOLOGICAL EXPERIMENTS, UNDER ARTICLE 8(2)(a)(ii)** 

The Prosecution will first establish each element of the war crime under
Article 8(2)(a)(ii), and then Thorn's liability under Article
25(3)(c).[^1] It will show that Jaku was a biological experiment to
which the Titans were subjected; that the experiment seriously
endangered their health; and that it was non-therapeutic, medically
unjustified and not carried out in their interest. It will then show
that the Titans were protected persons and that the conduct was
associated with an international armed conflict. Finally, it will show
that Thorn aided, abetted or otherwise assisted in the commission of the
crime with the required intent and knowledge.

### **Jaku was a biological experiment to which the Titans were subjected**

A biological experiment is such conduct whose primary purpose is to
study the as yet unknown effects of a product or situation on the human
body.[^2] The concept overlaps with the 'medical or scientific
experiments' prohibited by the Third and Fourth Geneva Conventions, and
all four Conventions list biological experiments as a grave breach.[^3]
The underlying norm behind the prohibition of biological experiment is
not confined to the Geneva Conventions alone; customary law likewise
prohibits medical or scientific experiment not indicated by a person's
health,[^4] and in line with article 21(3) of the Statute, human rights
law confirms that no one may be subjected 'without his free consent to
medical or scientific experimentation'.[^5] The prohibition, therefore,
turns on the character of the act done, not on the label its author
attaches to it. Post-World War II practice confirms that a medical or
military purpose does not change that character. In the Medical Trial,
the typhus experiments at Buchenwald were conducted to test vaccines for
the German armed forces; the Tribunal nevertheless convicted Rose, who
actively furthered them, and Handloser, the Chief of the Medical
Services, who failed to stop them.[^6] The Khabarovsk tribunal reached
the same conclusion about the Imperial Japanese Army's programme.[^7] An
experiment requires no laboratory design, control group or written
protocol. It is any systematic intervention that seeks generalisable
information about a human being, whether labelled 'biological' or
'medical'.[^8] Whether an intervention genuinely serves its stated
purpose is assessed objectively, by asking whether its 'design and
implementation are reasonable in relation to achieving its stated
objectives'.[^9] The prohibition exists to prevent protected persons
being used as guinea-pigs,[^10] and 'subjected' in Element 1 must be
read with Articles 12(2) and 50 of GC I, which protect the wounded and
sick from precisely this.[^11] Jaku meets this threshold. It was an
'experimental drug' in the 'early stages of development', declared
'ready for human trials'. Its stated aims included enhancing 'immunity'
and 'muscle strength and endurance' and putting Damaroi 'back on the
map' in biomedical science, and its results were measured in medical
exams that 'exceeded expectations' and 'surpass\[ed\] all initial
predictions'.[^12] Administering such a drug was necessarily a test of
its unknown effects, exactly as the typhus vaccines were. All 40
returning soldiers, four of them in comas, received the drug; by taking
it himself, Thorn became one more subject of the test.[^13] As such, the
Titans were subjected to a particular biological experiment.

### **The experiment seriously endangered the Titans' physical and mental health**

Element 2 requires that the experiment seriously endanger physical or
mental health or integrity; a concrete danger of serious harm suffices,
and the harm need not materialise.[^14] The drafters adopted the
threshold set out in Article 11(4) of Additional Protocol I. The danger
is judged objectively, by the foreseeable consequences in light of the
victims' state of health, so a body already weakened by disease is more
vulnerable to an untested drug, not less.[^15] The perpetrator need not
personally complete the value Judgment that the danger was
'serious'.[^16] Serious psychiatric disturbance qualifies as serious
harm to mental health, and it need not be permanent, provided it goes
beyond temporary unhappiness.[^17] No custody requirement limits this
element. Articles 12 and 50 of GC I contain none, and Additional
Protocol I itself requires that all the wounded and sick be respected
and protected 'to whichever Party they belong'; the 'power of the
adverse Party' condition of Article 11 cannot be imported into it.[^18]
The danger here was not only foreseeable but known. After the half
doses, many soldiers suffered 'severe headaches, disorientation and
episodes of paranoia'. The dose was nevertheless doubled on a bare
assurance that the symptoms were 'temporary', without notice to or
consent from the soldiers. The disorientation and paranoia persisted
after the injections ended, and by July all Titans reported paranoia,
several suffered increasing fatigue, and Thorn himself felt the drug's
benefits 'wearing off'.[^19] These were not the passing effects of a
cure; they outlasted the injections by three months. A sick patient's
health is seriously endangered when an untested drug is doubled despite
known neurological and psychiatric side effects. As such, the second
element is satisfied.

### **The Jaku experiment was non-therapeutic, medically unjustified and not carried out in the Titans' interest**

Element 3 requires that the experiment's intent was non-therapeutic and
that it was neither justified by medical reasons nor carried out in the
victims' interest. Article 12 of GC I prohibits biological experiments
on the wounded and sick outright.[^20] Article 13 of GC III, Article 32
of GC IV, and Article 11(1) of AP I express the same limitation that no
procedure may be performed unless it is indicated by the person's state
of health and is consistent with generally accepted medical
standards.[^21] The exception for new therapeutic methods is narrow. It
permits them only where they are justified on medical grounds and
dictated solely by the wish to improve the patient's condition.[^22]
Generally accepted standards are those reflected in the international
ethical guidelines on research involving human beings.[^23] Element 3
describes the character of the experiment, not the perpetrator's state
of mind; it is a material element in the form of a circumstance.[^24]
Footnote 46 of the Elements, although attached to 8(2)(b)(x), read with
Rule 92 of the ICRC Customary Study, as an interpretative aid, confirms
the content of the applicable medical standards,[^25] and the
perpetrator needs only to know the underlying facts, not conclude for
himself that the experiment was 'non-therapeutic'.[^26] Medical
justification is strictly confined. New remedies may be used only once
they have been 'satisfactorily proved to be innocuous', and only for
purely therapeutic purposes.[^27] Where a measure is said to be a
therapeutic necessity, that necessity must be 'convincingly shown to
exist'.[^28] Calling a procedure 'treatment' does not settle its
character. A surgeon who operates without anaesthetic treats his
patient, but treats him inhumanely, and biological experiments on the
wounded and sick violate the duty of humane treatment even where they
cause no death or serious injury.[^29] Nor can a doctor's duty to
preserve life excuse an experiment. That duty requires the State and its
doctors to provide care, while humane treatment is an obligation of
result that admits no exception.[^30] Consent cannot cure an experiment
of this kind. The Elements of Article 8(2)(a)(ii), unlike those of
Article 8(2)(b)(x), do not expressly exclude consent. But the wounded
and sick cannot validly consent to an experiment that endangers their
health, because consent can justify only treatment of an eminently
therapeutic nature.[^31] The Medical Trial made voluntary consent the
first condition of any lawful experiment. It held that the duty to
secure consent rests personally on each person who initiates, directs or
engages in the experiment, and that it cannot be delegated.[^32] Consent
must be informed, knowing and voluntary,[^33] and Additional Protocol I
prohibits such experiments even with consent.[^34] Even an unproven
intervention in clinical practice requires the informed consent of the
patient or a legally authorized representative, and treatment without
informed consent may itself be inhumane.[^35] Leading national decisions
confirm that patients must be told of material risks, that consent to
one procedure does not extend to another, and that an intervention
presented as treatment cannot conceal a research purpose.[^36] Even
where national law allows consent to be waived for service members, only
the President may do so, and only in writing.[^37] Measured against
these standards, Jaku's purpose was enhancement, not cure. The aim from
the outset was to make the Titans 'stronger' and 'even mightier than
last time', and Jaku was chosen because it would increase 'muscle
strength and endurance'. The dose was doubled 'for optimal results' only
after Thorn had felt 'newfound strength', and while some soldiers had
already recovered. The injections stopped once the group was 'deemed
fit' and its combat readiness 'exceptional', despite persisting side
effects, and the effect was later felt to be 'wearing off', as an
enhancement does and a cure does not.[^38] The measure of success was
combat fitness, not health. President Veyar's later public claim that
the treatment was 'for rehabilitation only' carries little weight
because it followed the leak and conflicts with the contemporaneous
description of the programme as a 'top secret matter of national
security'.[^39] Jaku had been proven neither innocuous nor necessary,
and nothing explains why the dose had to be doubled after some soldiers
had already recovered, other than Thorn's own 'newfound strength'.[^40]
Nor was there valid consent. The four comatose soldiers could not
consent. No soldier consented to the doubled dose, and nothing in the
record shows that the conscious soldiers were told Jaku was
experimental. Instead, *Nemo dat quod non habet* applies because Thorn's
approval 'on behalf of the Titans' could not supply consent that
belonged only to the individual soldiers, and no written waiver was
issued.[^41] As such, the third element is satisfied.

### **The Titans were protected persons, and Thorn was aware of the factual circumstances establishing that status**

The victims must be protected under one or more of the 1949 Geneva
Conventions; the Elements impose no requirement of custody or
control.[^42] GC I protects members of the armed forces of a Party to
the conflict who are wounded or sick,[^43] meaning persons who require
medical care due to disease or other disorder and who refrain from any
act of hostility.[^44] Whether a person is 'wounded or sick' is a matter
of common sense and good faith, and soldiers who remain symptomatic in
hospital are plainly 'sick'.[^45] That protection extends to a Party's
own soldiers. Article 12 of GC I protects the wounded and sick 'in all
circumstances' and without adverse distinction, and being 'in the power
of a Party' includes being in the power of one's own Party.[^46] Article
5 concerns persons who have 'fallen into the hands of the enemy', but
Article 13 contains no such condition, which confirms that not every
protected person must be in enemy hands.[^47] The Appeals Chamber has
accordingly recognized that the protection of GC I and GC II is not
confined to members of enemy forces, but covers the wounded and sick of
a Party's own armed forces.[^48] The Elements point the same way.
Footnote 33 does not define who is protected. It relaxes the mental
element 'with respect to nationality', so that the perpetrator needs
only to know that the victim belonged to an adverse party where
protected status turns on nationality, as it does under GC IV. Protected
status under GC I turns on the victim's condition, not his nationality,
and the ICTY has consistently read protected status by substance rather
than form, so as to give the Conventions their purpose of maximum
protection.[^49] *Expressio unius est exclusio alterius* applies because
the drafters expressly required that the victim be in the power of an
'adverse' or 'another' party in Articles 8(2)(b)(x)-2 and 8(2)(e)(xi)-2,
but deliberately omitted that requirement from Article 8(2)(a)(ii).[^50]
As to awareness, the perpetrator needs only to know the factual
circumstances establishing protected status; he need not make the legal
evaluation himself.[^51] The Titans fall squarely within this
protection. All 40 returning soldiers had contracted an unknown disease
with severe symptoms, and four were in induced comas when the injections
began; none was taking part in hostilities.[^52] Thorn knew all of this.
He handpicked and led the platoon, suffered the same illness, and told
the President that '\[s\]ome of the guys are holding on for their
lives'.[^53] As such, Elements 4 and 5 are satisfied.

### **The conduct took place in the context of and was associated with an international armed conflict, of which Thorn was aware**

Common Article 2 applies to any armed conflict between two or more High
Contracting Parties, even if one of them does not recognize a state of
war; no threshold of intensity is required.[^54] An international armed
conflict ('IAC') exists whenever there is a 'resort to armed force
between States',[^55] whatever the reasons for or the intensity of the
confrontation, and without any declaration or recognition; its existence
depends on the facts on the ground.[^56] It includes 'armed hostilities
between States through their respective armed forces'.[^57] Any
difference between two States that leads to the intervention of armed
forces constitutes an armed conflict, whatever its duration or the
number of victims,[^58] and it is irrelevant whether the State attacked
resists or is even aware of the intervention.[^59] The International
Court of Justice's distinction between an 'armed attack' and a 'mere
frontier incident' concerns the threshold for self-defense, not the
existence of an armed conflict.[^60] Once an IAC exists, IHL applies
throughout the territory of the parties, not only where fighting takes
place.[^61] The conduct must also be associated with the conflict. The
nexus is assessed objectively and does not require geographic or
temporal proximity to the fighting.[^62] It is met where the conflict
played a substantial part in the perpetrator's ability or decision to
commit the crime, the manner of its commission, or its purpose.[^63]
Relevant factors include whether the perpetrator was a combatant,
whether the act served the ultimate goal of a military campaign, and
whether it was committed as part of official duties.[^64] As to
awareness, the perpetrator needs only to know the facts indicating
hostilities; no legal evaluation of the existence or character of the
conflict is required.[^65] An IAC existed by mid-March 2025 at the
latest. Damaran soldiers, led by the Chief of the Damaran Defence Force
Thorn, crossed the River Targa into Pallavon on a mission to dismantle
its air defences so that Damaroi could 'take control of the country'. As
he withdrew, Thorn ordered five soldiers to continue the mission inside
Pallavon, and he set fire to the Pallavon riverbank for "easier access
next time". Those soldiers remained in Pallavon into early April, within
the charged period.[^66] This armed incursion by State forces into
another State's territory, carried out to prepare for its conquest,
constitutes an IAC, notwithstanding Pallovan's lack of armed
retaliation.[^67] The injections, although administered in Damaroi, fall
within the scope of that conflict. The injections were also associated
with the conflict. Thorn acted as Chief of the Damaran Defence Force.
The injections were intended to make the Titans 'stronger' for when 'we
go back in', and the squadron at once resumed training for Operation
Titan 2.0. With no word from the five soldiers left in Pallavon, the
operation was, so far as Thorn knew, still under way, and he had planned
and led the incursion himself.[^68] As such, Elements 6 and 7 are
satisfied.

### **Thorn aided, abetted or otherwise assisted in the commission of the crime with the required mental element**

Article 25(3)(c) makes a person liable as an accessory where he aids,
abets or otherwise assists in the commission, or attempted commission,
of a crime by another.[^69] It covers any form of assistance, physical
or moral, including approval and encouragement, provided for the purpose
of facilitating the crime.[^70] The assistance need not have a
substantial effect on the commission of the crime; it must be given with
the purpose of facilitating it and with awareness of it.[^71] 'Purpose'
refers to the purpose of facilitating the conduct rather than a desire
that the crime occur, so an accomplice need not wish for the outcome.
International courts have also rejected any further requirement that the
assistance be 'specifically directed' to the crime.[^72] The accessory's
liability depends on the commission of the material elements by the
principal, and the non-therapeutic character of an experiment is an
objective circumstance, not a matter of the principal's personal
belief.[^73] The material elements must be committed with intent and
knowledge;[^74] recklessness and dolus eventualis are excluded.[^75] The
post-World War tribunals likewise required intent as to the material
elements.[^76] In Milch, conviction required proof that the accused knew
of the experiments, knew that they were criminal in scope and execution,
and had that knowledge in time to act. Milch was acquitted because he
did not participate in the experiments, did not direct them, and did not
know of them.[^77] National law asks the same question of whether the
accomplice had advance knowledge of the facts at a time when he could
still refuse to take part.[^78] Applying these principles, Dr Loff was
the principal. She administered Jaku in a State-directed programme that
the President commissioned as a 'top secret matter of national
security', knowing that the drug was experimental and hoping that it
would enhance strength and restore Damaroi's scientific standing.[^79]
Thorn did not order the injections, but he approved them, knowing they
would be used for non-therapeutic purposes as a boost, which is conduct
squarely within Article 25(3)(c). His assistance was decisive at both
stages, so it would meet even a test of substantial effect. He agreed to
Jaku 'without hesitation' and proposed that it be given to 'the whole
group', and he later approved the doubled dose 'on behalf of the
Titans', after which Dr Loff moved to full injections.[^80] Without his
approval, neither phase of the experiment would have proceeded as it
did. Thorn is also the opposite of Milch. He knew that Jaku was
experimental, because the President passed on everything Dr Loff had
said, including that it was in the 'early stages of development' and
'ready for human trials'. He knew of the side effects before he approved
the higher dose. And he knew that no soldier had been asked to consent
to it, because he gave the approval himself. Each approval was given
with advance knowledge of these facts, at a time when he could still
have refused.[^81] *Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea* applies
because both the prohibited act and the requisite guilty mind are
present. Thus, there are substantial grounds to believe that Thorn is
individually criminally responsible under Article 25(3)(c) for the war
crime under Article 8(2)(a)(ii).

## **GENERAL THORN IS INDIVIDUALLY CRIMINALLY RESPONSIBLE UNDER ARTICLE 25(3)(b) FOR ORDERING, SOLICITING OR INDUCING THE WAR CRIME OF INTENTIONALLY DIRECTING ATTACKS AGAINST CIVILIAN OBJECTS UNDER ARTICLE 8(2)(b)(ii)**

The Prosecution will first establish that the strike of 22 July 2025
occurred in the context of and was associated with an international
armed conflict. It will then show that the three schools were civilian
objects, and that Damaran forces directed an attack against them,
intending them to be its object. It will next show that Thorn ordered
the attack or, alternatively, induced it. Finally, it will show that no
ground excludes his criminal responsibility.[^82]

###  **The contextual elements of Article 8(2)(b)(ii) are satisfied**

Elements 4 and 5 require that the conduct took place in the context of
and was associated with an international armed conflict, and that the
perpetrator was aware of the factual circumstances establishing it.[^83]
An IAC exists whenever there is a resort to armed force between States,
whatever its intensity.[^84] The conduct is associated with the conflict
where the conflict played a substantial part in the perpetrator's
decision, purpose, manner or ability to commit it.[^85] Awareness
requires knowledge of the facts, not a legal classification of the
conflict.[^86] The Prosecution has shown that an IAC existed by
mid-March 2025.[^87] In any event, its existence by 22 July 2025 is
beyond dispute. Damaran forces had seized Santo, killing 45 of its
defenders; Pallavon had ordered mass conscription; the Titans had
destroyed a Pallavon radar installation; and Damaroi had repeatedly
launched missiles from its own territory against Pallavon's training
facilities.[^88] The strike was closely associated with that conflict.
As the President's 'eyes and ears', Thorn gathered military intelligence
inside Pallavon, selected alleged enemy training sites, supplied their
coordinates to Damaran missile forces, and justified the strike as
preventing the schools' use 'against us'.[^89] The Damaran forces who
launched the barrage from Damaran territory against Pallavon knew the
facts establishing the conflict, as did Thorn, who led Damaran forces
inside Pallavon. As such, Elements 4 and 5 are satisfied.

###  **The three schools were civilian objects, not military objectives**

Element 2 requires that the object of the attack was a civilian object.
Under Article 52(2) of AP I, an object is a military objective only
where, by its nature, location, purpose or use, it makes an effective
contribution to military action and its total or partial destruction
offers a definite military advantage in the circumstances ruling at the
time. Both requirements are cumulative, and all other objects are
civilian objects.[^90] The contribution must exist in the actual
situation rather than in the abstract, so mere suitability for some
later military use is insufficient.[^91] 'Purpose' refers to an object's
intended future use, but that purpose must be concrete, specific to the
object attacked and objectively established at the time of the attack; a
general pattern or a prediction of future use cannot suffice.[^92] The
military advantage must likewise be concrete and perceptible, not
potential or speculative.[^93] Schools enjoy particular protection.
Article 52(3) of AP I provides that, in case of doubt whether an object
normally dedicated to civilian purposes, expressly including a school,
is being used to make an effective contribution to military action, it
shall be presumed not to be so used.[^94] Any separate claim of future
military purpose must then independently satisfy Article 52(2).
Buildings dedicated to education are separately protected by the Rome
Statute,[^95] and the Guidelines endorsed by more than 100 States
through the Safe Schools Declaration provide that schools must never be
destroyed in order to deprive the adversary of the ability to use them
in the future.[^96] The removal of civilians from the vicinity of
potential targets is a precaution IHL requires of the defender, not
evidence of military use.[^97] Finally, military necessity is reflected
in, and limited by, the definition of a military objective. Once an
object is civilian, no independent plea of necessity authorizes its
attack;[^98] the doctrine of Kriegsraison was rejected at
Nuremberg,[^99] and distinction between civilian objects and military
objectives is a 'cardinal' principle of IHL.[^100]

The final three schools fail both requirements of Article 52(2). As to
use, the record distinguishes them from the 17 schools previously
struck. Twelve had already been converted into military facilities, and
five further schools were struck only after the Titans had observed and
confirmed their conversion. By contrast, 'no positive action had yet
been taken' to use the final three for military purposes.[^101] The
President himself acknowledged that there was no confirmation that the
schools were actually being used as facilities, while Thorn conceded
that they might not be in use at present.[^102] The presumption in
Article 52(3) therefore applies with full force, and the dismissal of
pupils from the schools, a civilian precaution, cannot rebut it.

As to location, the schools' size, grounds, classrooms and proximity to
the railway showed at most their suitability for a possible future
conversion. If suitability sufficed, every large hospital or stadium
near a railway would be a lawful target.[^103] As to purpose, the
Cabinet's decision identified a category of schools, not these three.
Only 12 of the 20 qualifying schools were initially converted, and the
five later conversions occurred only after attacks on existing
facilities. That history shows a possibility of future conversion; it
does not show that the final three had already been assigned an actual
military purpose on 20 July, particularly as Pallavon had also converted
town halls into training camps.[^104] Hirgigo does not assist the
Defence. It concerned a power station under construction for that very
function, and even there the Commission required an effective
contribution as a 'first step',[^105] whereas no step towards conversion
had been taken at these schools. Nor did their destruction offer a
definite military advantage. Any asserted advantage depended on a chain
of future contingencies, as Pallavon would first have had to decide to
convert, equip, staff, and use the sites. Thorn confirmed that
contingency when he proposed their destruction 'before Pallavon has the
chance \[to\] use them against us'.[^106] That is preventive destruction
of civilian property in anticipation of its possible future use, which
is precisely the Kriegsraison reasoning that IHL rejects. As such, the
three schools were civilian objects and Element 2 is satisfied.

### **Damaran forces directed an attack against the schools, deliberately targeting them at Thorn's designated coordinates**

Element 1 requires that the perpetrator directed an attack. An 'attack'
is an act of violence against the adversary.[^107] Article 8(2)(b)(ii)
requires that an attack be directed at civilian objects, but it does not
require that every intended strike succeed.[^108] Element 3 requires
that the perpetrator intended the civilian objects to be the object of
the attack. Under Article 30, he must mean to engage in the conduct and
know the factual circumstances that make the objects civilian, but he
need not make the legal evaluation himself.[^109] Physical perpetrators
need not be identified by name. In *Brđanin* and *Krajišnik*, the ICTY
Appeals Chamber accepted that the persons involved in a common criminal
plan may be identified by category or group rather than
individually.[^110] *Id certum est quod certum reddi potest* applies
because a perpetrator is sufficiently identified when the facts make his
identity certain. Damaran forces launched the missile barrage from Como
at the coordinates Thorn supplied, directing an attack within Article
49(1) of AP I against all three schools.[^111] They were the intended
targets, not incidental damage falling under Article 8(2)(b)(iv).[^112]
The forces fired at coordinates identifying the schools themselves, so
the schools were the attack's intended object. Thorn's order identified
the targets as 'all remaining schools fitting the criteria', not as
confirmed military facilities, so those executing it knew they were
attacking schools.[^113] As such, Elements 1 and 3 are satisfied.

###  **Thorn is individually criminally responsible under Article 25(3)(b)**

Ordering under Article 25(3)(b) requires a person in a position of
authority to use that authority to instruct another to commit a crime.
The instruction must have a direct effect on the commission of the
crime, and the accused must at least be aware that the crime will occur
in the ordinary course of events.[^114] A formal superior--subordinate
relationship is not needed. Actual authority is enough, and it may be
inferred from circumstantial evidence.[^115] The crime must in fact
occur or at least be attempted.[^116] In the alternative, soliciting and
inducing cover urging, advising or otherwise influencing another person
to commit a crime. No superior--subordinate relationship is required, as
long as the influence has a direct effect on the commission of the
crime.[^117] Thorn ordered the attack. He was Chief of the Damaran
Defence Force, the President had entrusted him with intelligence and
planning, and he chose the targets himself. He 'gave the order to target
all remaining schools fitting the criteria, and provided strike
coordinates', and Damaran forces launched the missiles 'to coordinates
provided by Thorn'.[^118] His instruction went beyond general advice
because it named the targets and gave the coordinates needed to strike
them. The missiles hit all three schools, so the crime was committed.
Even an attempt would have been sufficient. Anyone who plans or decides
upon an attack must do everything feasible to verify that the target is
neither a civilian nor a civilian object, and must cancel or suspend the
attack if it becomes clear that the target is not a military
objective.[^119] Where there is doubt, only thoroughly verified
information will do.[^120] A failure to verify does not by itself prove
intent, but it can support that conclusion when read with other facts.
Thorn chose the schools knowing that no military use or conversion had
been confirmed. The President warned him of exactly this, yet Thorn went
ahead, reasoning that '\[e\]ven if they are not being used now, their
purpose is clear'.[^121] He also proposed a night strike. This shows
that he deliberately chose the schools themselves as targets. Calling it
a precaution does not turn civilian schools into military
objectives.[^122] Thorn also broke with the Titans' own practice, since
the five earlier schools were struck only after their conversion had
been confirmed. The maxim *acta exteriora indicant interiora secreta*
means that a person's outward actions reveal their inner intentions.
Thorn's actions show that he knew an attack on civilian objects would
occur in the ordinary course of events. In the alternative, Thorn
induced the strike. When the President questioned the attack, Thorn
pushed it forward. He insisted that the pattern was obvious, said why a
preventive strike was needed, suggested carrying it out at night and
supplied the coordinates. The missiles were launched within two days of
the schools stopping teaching, at the coordinates he provided. This
sequence shows that his influence had a direct effect on the commission
of the crime. There are therefore substantial grounds to believe that
Thorn ordered the attack or, in the alternative, induced it.

###  **No ground excludes Thorn's criminal responsibility**

A mistake of fact excludes responsibility only if it negates the mental
element,[^123] as where a commander, acting in good faith on erroneous
information, believes a civilian object to be a military objective;
under Rendulic, he is judged on the information then available.[^124] A
mistake of law likewise excludes responsibility only if it negates the
mental element.[^125] Article 31(1)(a)--(b) requires that mental
disease, defect or intoxication destroy the capacity to appreciate the
unlawfulness or nature of one's conduct, or to control it.[^126] Nothing
shows that Thorn relied on false intelligence that the final schools
were already converted or in military use. He knew such use was
unconfirmed and that no conversion had begun; his belief was a
prediction of future use, not misinformation about present facts.
Whether labelled factual, legal or mixed, it did not negate his
awareness of those decisive facts. Nor did Jaku destroy Thorn's
capacity. The general paranoia and fatigue was among the Titans, not
that Thorn's capacity was destroyed on 20 July. Weeks earlier, he had
reported that Jaku's benefits were 'wearing off'. His structured target
assessment, response to the President, night-time timing and provision
of coordinates show goal-directed decision-making, and his weighing of
civilian risk shows he appreciated the operation's nature and
consequences. The President's remark that the plan 'doesn't sound like
you' came after the strike and concerned a different operation. There
are therefore substantial grounds to believe that Thorn is individually
criminally responsible under Article 25(3)(b) for the war crime under
Article 8(2)(b)(ii).
